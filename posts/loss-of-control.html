Board Member Warns of Catastrophic Loss of Control

Paul Christiano says the company is “not on track” to reduce catastrophic risk to acceptable levels — the sharpest inside warning to date.

Paul Christiano — OpenAI board member and US government technology adviser — said the company is “not on track to reduce risk of catastrophic loss of control to an acceptable level.”

Why it matters

The sharpest warning yet comes from inside the boardroom. Treat control evals as a gating dependency, not a checkbox.

Warnings from outside researchers are routine; warnings from the board are not. Christiano sits at the intersection of company governance and the government's AI safety apparatus, so his assessment reads as both internal audit and policy signal.

The core concern

Control evaluations: can the company reliably detect, constrain, and shut down highly capable agents with incentives to evade oversight? His answer, in effect: not yet — and capability is moving faster than control.

What follows

OpenAI points to the Astra safety card, staged deployment, and expanded red-teaming while acknowledging the control problem is unsolved. Expect the warning to surface in forthcoming congressional hearings on frontier-model oversight — and in every enterprise risk review this quarter.
