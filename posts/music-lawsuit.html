Music Industry Sues Anthropic Over Training Data

A 48-page complaint naming founders personally, seeking up to $150,000 per song. The case that could redraw training-data law.

Sony Music Publishing and Warner Chappell filed a 48-page complaint against Anthropic, alleging copyrighted lyrics trained models without permission — and naming founders personally.

Why it matters

Lyrics may produce the first clean fair-use precedent on training data. Price licensing risk into every training run until it lands.

The personal-naming strategy is the tell: publishers want to establish that training-data decisions were knowing executive choices, not engineering accidents. The statutory ask — up to $150,000 per song — puts exposure in the hundreds of millions.

Why this case matters

Training-data litigation has mostly settled or stalled. A fully litigated publisher-vs-lab case on lyrics — short, creative, easily memorized texts — could produce the first clean precedent on whether training on copyrighted text is fair use, and what remedies follow if it isn't.

A publisher win reprices every frontier training run; a lab win cements scrape-and-train. Either way, licensing markets reprice on the verdict. Watch for a settlement with a headline licensing deal — the industry's preferred ending.
